# Three things I learned about LILA BLACK using this tool

All figures below come from the tool's own preprocessing step (`public/data/analysis.json`,
regenerated by `npm run preprocess`) and are reproducible in the UI. Grid stats use a 32×32
cell grid (1,024 cells) over each map's logical space.

> **Data caveat first (because it shapes every insight):** this export is a **partial sample** —
> 1,243 journey files across 796 matches (≈1.6 files/match), and **743 of 796 matches (93%) contain
> only a single recorded player**. Only 52 matches have 5–16 players. So *per-match* rosters are
> incomplete, but the **aggregate spatial patterns** (where people go, fight, loot, die) pool 89,104
> events and are the trustworthy signal. I lead with spatial conclusions, not per-match ones.

---

## 1. Combat is PvE, not PvP — bots are the entire threat model

**What caught my eye:** turn on the **Deaths** heatmap and toggle event markers — the map fills with
red *"Death (by bot)"* triangles and green *"Kill (bot)"* triangles, but the *"Kill/Death (by player)"*
shapes almost never appear.

**Evidence:** of **741 human deaths**, **699 (94.3%) were caused by bots**, **39 (5.3%) by the storm**,
and **just 3 (0.4%) by other players.** Offensively, humans scored **2,410 bot-kills vs 3 player-kills**
(a 803:1 ratio). This holds across all 5 days and all 3 maps.

**Likely cause:** humans rarely share an instance in this sample (see the 93% single-player-match note),
so player-vs-player contact is almost mechanically impossible here.

**Actionable / metrics affected:** if PvP is a design pillar, this is a red flag to validate against the
*full* telemetry. Track **PvP-kill rate, time-to-first-human-contact, and humans-per-instance**. Because
essentially all combat is scripted, **bot spawn/patrol placement _is_ the combat design** — where you put
bots is where the fights are (and those locations are visible in the Kills heatmap).

**Why a Level Designer cares:** you can stop guessing where firefights happen and place/tune bots to author
combat intentionally, rather than hoping players collide.

---

## 2. Looting dominates play and concentrates on a handful of magnet POIs

**What caught my eye:** with markers on, blue *Loot* diamonds blanket the map — far more than any other
event — and they pile up in tight clusters. Switch to the **Kills** heatmap and the hottest kill cells sit
*on top of* the busiest loot cells.

**Evidence:** **Loot = 12,866 events**, more than **4× all combat events combined** (~3,154). Loot is highly
concentrated: on Ambrose Valley the single hottest cell (world ≈ `x -47, z -346`) logged **583 pickups —
~3× the 12th-ranked hotspot (197)**. Kill and loot hotspots **co-locate**: Ambrose cell ≈ `x 94, z -65`
ranks in the **top 3 for both** kills and loot; `x 38, z 244` appears in the top ranks of **kills, deaths,
and loot** simultaneously.

**Actionable / metrics affected:** these are your **"hot drop" POIs** — loot draws players, players die where
they loot. Track **loot-per-POI, kills-per-POI, and a contested-POI ratio.** The 583-pickup site is a magnet
worth auditing (over-rich?), while cold POIs can be enriched to redistribute traffic and spread combat.

**Why a Level Designer cares:** it turns "which POIs matter" from opinion into a ranked list, and shows which
loot placements are actually generating the fights you want.

---

## 3. Players use less than half of every map — and the storm isn't pushing them

**What caught my eye:** the **Traffic** heatmap glows in a compact central core on every map while the edges
stay dark; the water and periphery on Lockdown are essentially untouched.

**Evidence:** measured coverage (visited cells / 1,024):
**Ambrose Valley 43.3%** (581 dead cells), **Grand Rift 36.7%** (648 dead), **Lockdown 32.5%** (691 dead).
**More than half of every map is never entered.** Meanwhile the storm — the mechanic meant to compress play
space — caused only **39 deaths (5.3%)**, scattered one-per-cell, i.e. it rarely catches anyone.

**Actionable / metrics affected:** track **map-coverage %, edge-region traffic, avg distance-from-center, and
storm-death rate.** Two levers: (a) **reclaim/repurpose** the 55–67% unused area (a lot of art/design budget is
never seen — or simply shrink the playable footprint), and (b) use **storm pathing and objective/loot placement
to pull players outward** into the cold regions instead of letting them nest in the core.

**Why a Level Designer cares:** you're building (and paying for) map that players never see, and the storm — your
main tool to counter that — currently has almost no behavioural effect. This quantifies both problems and points
to the specific cold regions to fix.

---

### Supporting context
- **Map popularity is lopsided:** Ambrose Valley **71.1%** of matches (566), Lockdown **21.5%** (171),
  Grand Rift **7.4%** (59) — so balancing effort should be weighted toward Ambrose.
- **Match records are short bursts:** recorded `ts` spans ~**0.4 s** per match (median), so the tool's playback
  is time-scaled to a comfortable wall-clock window (see ARCHITECTURE → timestamps).
